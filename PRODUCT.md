# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: hiring managers and recruiters evaluating Roma Shuliatiev for a full-time
product-designer role. They arrive skimming, often between other tabs, looking to
answer "is this person a fit?" quickly, then decide whether to read deeper or reach
out. Secondary: design leads and peers who will scrutinize craft and process once a
candidate clears the first skim. The site must satisfy the skim without insulting the
close read.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio at romamakes.com that earns a hiring conversation. It presents a
product designer with a developer background through selected work, measurable
outcomes, and working artifacts (e.g. a white-label eSIM prototype built on the real
production stack). Success is a qualified visitor leaving with quiet confidence in the
craft and reaching out: email, LinkedIn, or CV download. The design is itself a work
sample, so the site's own build quality is part of the evidence.

## Brand Personality

Understated, precise, evidence-led. Calm authority that lets the work and the numbers
carry the claim instead of adjectives. Reads like a considered editorial page, not a
sales pitch: short, plain sentences; proof before assertion; comfortable with
restraint and with admitting what the work actually was ("mostly protecting the simple
flow people trust"). Confident without volume.

## Anti-references

- **Loud SaaS landing pages.** No gradient heroes, animated metric counters, glowing
  CTAs, or feature-grid cards. The startup-template look undercuts the evidence-led tone.
- **Generic portfolio templates.** No Framer/Webflow defaults, identical project cards,
  or "I'm a passionate creative" framing.
- **Dribbble showpieces.** No style-over-substance mockups floating on gradients; every
  visual ties to a real problem and outcome.
- **Over-animated / flashy sites.** No scroll-jacking, gratuitous parallax, or motion
  that performs rather than serves comprehension.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the work, don't sell it.** Lead with evidence: real numbers, real artifacts,
   the actual problem. Let proof precede any claim. Cut adjectives that do the selling.
2. **Craft is the argument.** The site's own build quality is an exhibit. Typography,
   spacing, motion, and code are judged as portfolio work, so hold them to that bar.
3. **Respect the skim, reward the read.** Hiring managers scan first. Make the top-level
   signal legible in seconds; layer depth for those who go further. Never bury the lede.
4. **Restraint is the brand.** Quiet, typographic, near-monochrome with one deliberate
   accent. Nothing decorative that doesn't earn its place. When in doubt, remove it.
5. **Designer who codes.** The differentiator is closing the gap between design and what
   engineers ship. Favor working prototypes and production-faithful detail over static
   pictures of work.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort, not a formal commitment. Aim for legible contrast in both light and dark
themes, keyboard-reachable interactive elements, and honoring `prefers-reduced-motion`
(already wired via `motion-reduce:` utilities). No pledge to a specific WCAG conformance
level; treat the above as defaults, not a compliance checklist.
